# Radial Components of the Idelic Quotient and Zero Modes

## Manuscript weights and dual action

The pinned Masullo PDF is the 2026-11-20 edition with SHA-256 `e039d3f6cf0a4b14f03c7edb85097752dd65c0a2fe543c76fe992be234f97bf5`. In equations (9.2.1.30)–(9.2.1.31) on p. 410, take the symmetric strip $I=[-\alpha,\alpha]$ and Sobolev order $s=2$. The substitution $x=\log t$ gives the radial weight of the original space.

$$
w_+(x)=\max(e^{-2\alpha x},e^{2\alpha x})(1+x^2)
       =e^{2\alpha|x|}(1+x^2).
$$

The reciprocal weight $w_-=w_+^{-1}$ of the dual space equals $w_\alpha$ from the earlier Atlas model. The Lean theorem `radial_dual_weight_eq` verifies this identity. We use $dt/t=dx$. For the trivial character, the Haar normalization of the norm-one kernel is absorbed into the kernel and pairing.

Equation (10.2.2.2) on p. 445 realizes a fixed character component as a quotient by a closed subspace $W$. Equation (10.2.2.3) pairs the original space with its dual by integrating the product. In logarithmic coordinates this is $\langle f,g\rangle_{\mathrm{bil}}=\int f(x)g(x)\,dx$. The dual action of $f(x-y)$ is therefore $g(x+y)$, with no conjugation. The Lean theorem `radial_translation_pairing` verifies the change of variables. The Hilbert-space adjoint uses a sesquilinear pairing on the same space. Confusing the two pairings can change the conjugation and translation sign.

## Application assuming the annihilator is generated by zero modes

Let $H_\pm=L^2(w_\pm\,dx)$. We consider the fixed trivial character on a strip of positive width. Under the density conclusion of manuscript Theorem 10.2.2.1 on p. 446, the annihilator of $W$ equals the closed span $E$.

$$
E=\overline{\operatorname{span}\{x^j e^{zx}: z\text{ is the centered coordinate of a retained zero},\
                              0\le j<n(z)\}}\subset H_-.
$$

Inside the open strip, $|\Re z|<\alpha$, exponential decay puts every finite-degree mode in $H_-$. At the strip boundary with $s=2$, the integrability test is $2j-2<-1$, so only $j=0$ is retained. This agrees with the manuscript's multiplicity truncation in this case.

Applying $U_yg(x)=g(x+y)$ to a mode gives $e^{zy}(x+y)^j e^{zx}$. The binomial theorem keeps the result within degrees $0,\ldots,j$ for the same $z$. Order the modes at each zero by increasing degree. Every finite span in this order is invariant. The boundedness of $U_y$ extends invariance to the closed span $E$. Lean verifies the binomial action and the preservation of the closed span by a bounded operator.

The weight comparison from the previous stage gives the same translation constant for $w_+$. Hence the original kernel action $C^-_kf(x)=\int k(y)f(x-y)\,dy$ and the dual action $C^+_kg(x)=\int k(y)g(x+y)\,dy$ are bounded on their respective spaces. We use $k\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$. The weighted $L^1$ condition from the previous stage also suffices. The dual action preserves $E$. Fubini and the bilinear pairing give the following identity.

$$
\langle C_k^- f,g\rangle_{\mathrm{bil}}
 =\langle f,C_k^+g\rangle_{\mathrm{bil}}=0
 \qquad (f\in W,\ g\in E)
$$

Density and closedness of $W$ imply that the annihilator of $E$ is $W$, so $C_k^-W\subseteq W$. Thus $C_k^-$ descends to a bounded operator on the quotient. This analytic application assumes the density conclusion of manuscript Theorem 10.2.2.1.

## Quotient operators and window projections

Lean verifies that a bounded operator with $TW\subseteq W$ induces $[x]\mapsto[Tx]$ and satisfies $\|\overline T\|\le\|T\|$. Via the Hilbert-space isometry between $H/W$ and $W^\perp$, $\overline T$ corresponds to $P_{W^\perp}T|_{W^\perp}$. The pullback of continuous linear functionals and preservation of the annihilator by the adjoint were also verified.

For the orthogonal projection $P_N$ onto a finite mode window, Lean gives $\|P_NTP_N\|\le\|T\|$. In finite dimension each singular value is bounded by the operator norm, so the trace norm is at most $\operatorname{rank}(P_N)\|T\|$. This last inequality is analytic. Dimension-independent trace norm bounds and convergence of the manuscript's biorthogonal partial sums require further estimates.

## Resolvent construction

First prove strong continuity of $U_y$ on the dense subspace $C_c(\mathbb R)$. The uniform operator bound around zero extends this to the weighted space. For $b>\omega=\alpha+1/2$, define

$$
R_b g=\int_0^\infty e^{-by}U_yg\,dy
$$

as a Bochner integral with $\|R_b\|\le (b-\omega)^{-1}$. Shifting the integration variable and taking the right difference quotient at zero gives $(bI-A)R_bg=g$. Integration by parts on the generator domain gives the other inverse identity. This construction is analytic.

Lean verifies that the Laplace integral gives $(b-z)^{-1}e^{zx}$ on an exponential mode. In the invariant flag supplied by the polynomial-mode ordering, the resolvent diagonal on each one-dimensional quotient is $(b-z)^{-1}$. The public repair proposal then bounds the Hilbert–Schmidt column sum using generator energy and a zero count. Applying it requires the weighted generator and basis construction, the manuscript's density input, and control of the infinite sums. The geometric trace comparison remains open.
